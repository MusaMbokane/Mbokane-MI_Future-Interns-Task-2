# Mbokane-MI_Future-Interns-Task-2
Future Interns Task 2 | Customer Retention &amp; Churn Analysis | Power BI

# Project Overview
This project focuses on looking at customers' behaviour and what makes them stay or leave. I have used PowerBI to complete the task and create the dashboard that will basically help the business track their customers' behaviours. The main goal of the project is to figure out why customers stop doing business with the company and find ways to keep them from leaving, which can help reduce losses and improve retention. 

The dashboard provides a clear overview of key performance indicators (KPIs) such as total number of customers, churn rate in percentage, average tenure, and revenue loss, and it also breaks down these numbers into groups of customers, so the company can see how different types of customers are doing.

# Business Questions Addressed 
The dashboard is designed to answer the following key business questions:

- What is the overall customer churn rate?
- Which customer segments are most likely to churn?
- How does churn vary by payment method, contract type, and tenure?
- What is the financial impact of customer churn (revenue loss)?
- Are there noticeable trends in churn over time or tenure?
- Do demographic factors, for example gender, influence churn behavior?

# Data and Data Preparation
The data used was downloaded from Kaggle site as: WA_Fn-UseC_-Telco-Customer-Churn.csv.

To get good results, the data had to be cleaned up and prepaired first. This involved several steps which will explained below, this was done to make sure that the information is accurate and useful: 

- Data Cleaning: Removed duplicates with Excel and Power query, handled missing values, and corrected inconsistences
- Data Transformation: Converted data types and standardized categorical variables
- Feature Engineering: Create calculated fields such as churn rate, tenure groups, and revenue loss
- Data Modelling: Established relationships between tables for efficient analysis
- DAX Measures: Developed key metrics including total customers, churn rate(%), average tenure, and revenue loss using PowerBI

# Key Insights
- The overall churn rate is 26.54%, indicating a significant loss of customers.
- Customers using electronic check payments exhibit the highest churn rate.
- Month-to-month contracts have the highest number of churned customers when compared to longer-term contracts' customers.
- Churn is significantly higher among customers with shorter tenure, especially in early months.
- Gender distribution is nearly equal, suggesting no strong correlation between gender and churn.
- Customers with short-term commitments are more likely to leave, highlighting the importance of long-term contracts
- Early customer experience plays a critical role in retention, as churn drops with increased tenure
- Payment behavior is a strong churn indicator, with certain payment methods linked to higher risk
- The business is experiencing notable revenue loss due to churn, emphasizing the need for retention strategies

This insights show that a lot of customers are leaving the services of the company, with a churn rate of 26.54%. This is a big problem, and it is likely because customers are not getting what they want. Maybe they are not having a good experience when they first sign up, or maybe the service is not living up to their expectations. It is also possible that other companies are offering better services, which is making customers switch. When customers don't see the benefits of a service right away, they are more likely to cancel their subscription, which is making the churn rate even higher. This is something that needs to be addressed, because if customers are not finding value in the service, they are not going to stick around.

People who use electronic checks to pay for things tend to stop using a service quickly. This might be because they don't feel as tied to the service and like to be able to change their minds easily. Also, paying with electronic checks can be a hassle because it often requires someone to process the payment by hand, which can be slower and more annoying than using a credit card. These customers are also sensitive to prices, so if they find a better deal somewhere else, they'll probably switch to that instead.

When you look at the analysis, it is clear that customers who are on month-to-month contracts are more likely to stop using a service. This is because they don't have a long-term commitment, so they can leave whenever they want without having to pay any penalties. On the other hand, contracts that last for a longer period often come with perks like discounts or extra benefits, which give customers a reason to stick around. But with month-to-month plans, there is nothing to keep customers from leaving, which makes it more likely that they wil stay.

# Findings
Customers with short-term commitments are more likely to leave, highlighting the importance of long-term contracts. Early customer experience plays a critical role in retention, as churn drops with increased tenure. Payment behavior is a strong churn indicator, with certain payment methods linked to higher risk. The business is experiencing notable revenue loss due to churn, emphasizing the need for retention strategies.
